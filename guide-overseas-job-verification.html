How to verify an overseas job offer before you pay, submit originals or travel.

A real overseas vacancy should survive basic verification. This guide shows a practical order of checks for the job, foreign employer, Recruiting Agent, contract, fees and communication trail—without assuming that a logo, WhatsApp message or offer-letter PDF is proof by itself.

This article explains a verification method. It does not confirm any specific employer, visa or Recruiting Agent. Vacancy-specific facts must be checked against the actual documents and authorized process.

Most bad overseas-recruitment decisions happen because several different questions are treated as one question. A candidate sees a job title, salary, company name and urgent deadline and asks, “Is this job genuine?” But genuine overseas recruitment is not one fact. It is a chain: the vacancy must be real, the employer must be real, the recruitment route must be authorized, the contract must match the promise, the payment request must be legitimate, and the travel documents must belong to the same process.

A useful verification routine therefore does not begin with “Do I trust this person?” It begins with “What can I independently verify?” The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) advises candidates to verify the Recruiting Agent, job and employer, and warns against handing over money or important documents before genuineness is established. The checks below turn that principle into a repeatable process.

1. First identify the recruitment route

Before checking salary or benefits, establish who is legally responsible for the overseas recruitment. For Indian citizens, overseas employment may be arranged through a registered Recruiting Agent, directly by a foreign employer where the applicable permit/process exists, or through another legally permitted route. A sourcing or coordination business is not automatically the same thing as a registered Recruiting Agent.

Where law requires a registered Recruiting Agent or authorized foreign-employer process, that regulated responsibility remains with the concerned authorized party.

Ask for the name of the responsible Recruiting Agent or direct foreign-employer route. If someone avoids this question and says “everything is managed internally,” that is not a substitute for authorization.

2. Verify the vacancy as a complete set of terms

A vacancy should be more than a poster. Write down the details you were actually offered: employer or project, country and work location, job title, number of positions, salary, duty hours, overtime rule if stated, food/accommodation/transport terms, contract period, leave, probation, interview method and required documents.

Now compare every later document against that first written set. Fraud and poor recruitment often reveal themselves through drift: the salary changes after selection, the job title changes on the visa or contract, the promised accommodation becomes an allowance, or the employer name in the offer letter differs from the company named during the interview.

3. Check the Recruiting Agent—not only the agency name

MEA states that Recruiting Agents conducting overseas recruitment from India are required to be registered under the Emigration Act, 1983. Candidates can check registered Recruiting Agents through the Government of India's eMigrate system. Do not rely only on a visiting card, GST registration, MSME/Udyam registration, social-media page or company incorporation record; those may verify a business exists, but they do not by themselves prove authorization to act as a Recruiting Agent for overseas employment.

Ask for the Recruiting Agent's registration details and verify the current status using the official route. Also check whether the vacancy is actually being handled under that RA's process. A genuine RA number copied from another business is still misuse.

RA check

  • Get the full legal name of the Recruiting Agent.
  • Get the registration/RC details used for the recruitment.
  • Verify the RA through official MEA/eMigrate information.
  • Confirm the person contacting you is actually connected to that RA.
  • Ask for a written link between the RA and the specific employer/demand when appropriate.

4. Verify the foreign employer independently

Do not stop after verifying the recruiter. The employer must also be checked. Look for a consistent legal name, real operating address, working corporate contact details and a business presence that makes sense for the role. If you have an offer letter, use contact information obtained independently—not only the phone number printed on the same PDF—to ask whether the offer or recruitment is genuine.

MEA's intending-emigrant guidance specifically recommends cross-checking the foreign employer and, when in doubt, verifying the job through the concerned Indian Mission/Post. This is especially important when the employer name is unfamiliar, the job is unusually generous, or the recruitment bypasses a normal interview.

Be careful with “project names.” A project can be real while the recruiting claim is false. The useful question is not “Does this airport/hospital/refinery exist?” but “Is this employer or contractor actually recruiting these workers through this stated channel?”

5. Read the employment contract as the final commercial truth

The contract should make the important terms understandable before departure. Compare it with the vacancy and interview promise. Focus on the basic salary, allowances, working hours, weekly rest, overtime treatment, accommodation/food/transport, leave, contract period, probation, termination terms, ticket and any deductions.

Do not sign a document you cannot read or have not had explained. If a bilingual contract is used, ask which version controls if the texts differ. If blank spaces remain in a document, do not assume they will be filled later in your favour.

A simple ruleIf a term matters enough to influence your decision—salary, duty hours, accommodation, job title, location—try to get it in the final written employment documentation. “The company will manage it later” is not equivalent to a written term.

6. Verify payment separately from the job

A genuine vacancy does not automatically make every payment request genuine. Treat payment as its own verification step. Ask who is receiving the money, what exact service it is for, whether the amount is permitted, what receipt will be issued, what happens if the service is not delivered, and whether the payment account belongs to the responsible business or authorized process.

MEA publishes fee guidance and advises candidates to obtain receipts. Because fee rules can change and can depend on the recruitment route, check the current official guidance rather than relying on an old screenshot or social-media post. Do not pay simply because someone says a visa will expire in an hour or a slot will be cancelled immediately.

For any AVC service fee, the service purpose and price should be stated separately from official government, medical-center, Wafid or other third-party charges. A third-party fee should never be silently presented as AVC revenue.

7. Protect passport and identity documents

Overseas recruitment naturally requires identity documents, but timing and destination matter. Do not send your passport, Aadhaar, bank details, educational certificates, OTPs or signatures to an unknown personal account simply because a vacancy looks attractive.

  • Share only the document actually required at the current stage.
  • Use the official application or authorized business route where available.
  • Watermark copies for a specific purpose when appropriate and accepted.
  • Never share passwords, card PINs, banking login credentials or OTPs.
  • Keep a copy of everything you submit.

If an original passport is required for a formal process, note who received it, why, on what date and under whose responsibility. A casual handover without acknowledgement is avoidable risk.

8. Build a record trail before there is a problem

Good record keeping is not only for complaints. It helps you spot inconsistencies early. Save the original vacancy, application reference, employer/RA details, interview message, offer letter, contract, payment receipt, medical record, visa-related documents and important WhatsApp/email conversations.

Rename files clearly—for example, 2026-08-20-offer-employer-name.pdf—instead of keeping twenty files called “document.pdf”. If a term changes, ask for the change in writing. A written record makes it easier to compare versions and easier to explain your case to an employer, RA, government office or support service if something goes wrong.

9. Use a go/no-go decision instead of a feeling

Before paying a significant amount, handing over originals or buying a ticket, stop and score the process. Can you answer each of these questions with evidence?

Final go/no-go checklist

  1. Who is the actual foreign employer?
  2. Who is the legally responsible Recruiting Agent or authorized recruitment route?
  3. Can I verify that party independently?
  4. Do the job title, salary and benefits match across documents?
  5. Did a real selection/interview process take place where expected?
  6. Do I understand the contract before signing?
  7. Is every requested payment explained, receipted and payable to the right party?
  8. Are my passport and identity documents being handled through a traceable process?
  9. Do I have copies of the important records?

If several answers are “no,” the correct response is not to move faster. It is to verify first. Urgency is common in recruitment, but urgency is never evidence of genuineness.
