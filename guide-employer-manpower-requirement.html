Employer guide / manpower sourcing

A good manpower requirement is a screening specification, not a poster headline.

When salary, duty, experience, documents and interview criteria are scattered across chats, candidate quality drops and rework rises. A structured brief gives sourcing teams a single version of the requirement to screen against.

A manpower request such as “Need 50 electricians, urgent, Gulf salary good” can produce activity, but it cannot produce controlled screening. The sourcing team must guess what kind of electrician is required, what experience matters, which documents are mandatory and which package candidates are expected to accept.

A written specification reduces those guesses. It also protects the employer: candidates can be briefed against the same terms, interview teams see the same criteria, and later changes can be identified instead of disappearing into chat history.

1. Why manpower briefs fail

Most weak requirements have one of four problems. They omit critical terms, mix mandatory and preferred criteria, change after sourcing begins, or do not identify who owns each later stage. The result is predictable: too many irrelevant CVs, candidates withdrawing after salary clarification, repeated interviews and disputes over what was originally promised.

One version of truthBefore sourcing starts, agree a written requirement version. When a term changes, issue a revised version rather than editing old chat messages silently.

2. Start with employer and project identity

State the legal employer or responsible company name and, when useful, the project/client/worksite. These are not always the same entity. If candidates will ultimately be employed by a contractor but work at a client site, make that distinction clear.

Include country, city/region and work environment. “Oman” is less useful than “Muscat municipality cleaning project” when the project context affects duties, accommodation, transport or interview expectations.

Identity fields

  • Employer/legal entity
  • Project or client, if public/appropriate to disclose
  • Country and work location
  • Contract/project duration if relevant
  • Responsible Recruiting Agent/authorized recruitment route for India
  • Whether employer/RA identity may be shown publicly or only during verified processing

3. Define the job by duties, not only title

Job titles vary across countries and industries. “Helper,” “technician,” “sales executive” or “mechanic” can mean very different work. List the core duties that make a candidate suitable.

For a cable jointer, specify voltage range, termination/jointing type, certification or test expected. For a retail salesperson, specify product category, language, POS/stock duties and sales target environment. For a driver, specify vehicle class, licence expectations, route type and whether GCC experience is mandatory or preferred.

If several roles share the same package, keep their screening criteria separate. A single poster can advertise them together, but the sourcing matrix should not.

4. Separate mandatory criteria from preferences

CategoryMandatory examplePreferred example
ExperienceMinimum 3 years in industrial maintenanceGCC refinery experience
LanguageBasic spoken EnglishArabic conversation

Trade evidenceWorking video + interviewITI certificate

This separation prevents good candidates from being rejected because a preference was accidentally treated as a rule. It also prevents sourcing teams from flooding the employer with profiles that miss a true mandatory condition.

5. Write compensation so candidates can understand the real monthly proposition

State basic salary separately from fixed allowances, variable incentives and overtime. If food is provided, say “food provided.” If an allowance is provided instead, state the amount. Do the same for accommodation and transport.

Duty hours should be written as normal hours and break pattern, with overtime described separately where known. A line such as “10 hours duty” should clarify whether that includes breaks and whether additional overtime can occur.

Package fields

  • Basic salary and currency
  • Fixed allowance(s)
  • Incentive/commission formula if applicable
  • Normal duty hours and weekly rest
  • Overtime rule/rate where confirmed
  • Accommodation: provided or allowance
  • Food: provided, allowance or self
  • Transport
  • Medical/insurance responsibilities
  • Ticket terms if part of the offer
  • Contract duration and probation where known

Do not combine uncertain overtime with guaranteed salary to create a higher headline. Candidates should know which part is fixed.

6. Specify how suitability will be tested

Screening works best when the evidence matches the role. A CV may be enough for an initial shortlist, but some trades need a work video, technical interview, practical trade test, portfolio or licence copy.

Define the interview method: Zoom/Teams/WhatsApp video, employer call, physical interview, trade test, or CV selection. Give expected interview dates and time zone. If candidates must travel to a city for selection, state who bears travel cost and what they should bring.

For work videos, state the exact task to show. “Send working video” produces inconsistent material. “Show spray-paint surface preparation and gun handling” or “show reversing/parking a light vehicle safely” produces more comparable evidence.

7. Add a responsibility map before candidates are sourced

For overseas recruitment from India, sourcing is only one part. Clarify who is responsible for final selection, employment documentation, visa/work authorization, emigration clearance where applicable, medical instruction, ticketing and deployment.

Government of India/MEA rules govern registered Recruiting Agents and direct foreign-employer recruitment routes. If a sourcing partner such as AVC is involved, the brief should still identify the registered/authorized party responsible for regulated overseas recruitment.

Role: [job title]
Headcount: [number]

Language: [required/preferred]
Basic salary: [amount/currency]
Allowances/incentive: [separate]
Duty: [hours/break/rest]
Accommodation: [provided/allowance]
Food: [provided/allowance/self]
Transport: [provided/allowance]
Interview method/date: [details]
Evidence required: [CV/video/certificate/test]
Responsible RA/authorized route: [details]
Target joining/deployment window: [date/window]
Public disclosure restrictions: [employer/project/contact details]

Once this brief is agreed, the sourcing team can build a candidate checklist from it and report meaningful funnel numbers: profiles received, mandatory criteria passed, interviews scheduled, selections and pending documents.
