Safety guide / overseas recruitment

Recruitment fraud usually asks you to trust the message before you verify the process.

Copied logos, real company names and professional PDFs can all be used in false recruitment. Use a repeatable verification routine that checks identity, authorization, vacancy terms, payment and documents separately.

No single red flag proves fraud. The purpose is to trigger independent verification before money, originals or sensitive data move.

Overseas recruitment is attractive to scammers because the candidate often cannot physically visit the employer, the process naturally involves documents and payments, and genuine recruitment can move quickly. That combination makes urgency feel normal.

The safest approach is not to memorise every scam. Learn the structure of a legitimate process and notice when one part cannot be independently checked.

1. A common fraud pattern: authority → urgency → isolation → payment

Many suspicious approaches follow the same sequence. First, the sender borrows authority: a known company name, an agency logo, a government-looking seal or a photo of a real office. Then urgency appears: “visa closes today,” “last two seats,” “pay before 5 PM.” Next, the candidate is discouraged from checking with other people. Finally, payment or sensitive documents are requested.

The best interruption point is between authority and urgency. Stop and verify through a contact route you found independently.

2. Identity red flags

  • The WhatsApp display name matches a company, but the number is not published by that company.
  • An email uses a free domain or a spelling that imitates the real company domain.
  • The sender refuses a video call or official email confirmation for a major transaction.
  • A real Recruiting Agent registration number is displayed, but the business name/contact details do not match.
  • Company registration, GST or MSME documents are shown as proof of Recruiting Agent authorization even though they are different registrations.
  • The sender asks you not to contact the employer or registered RA directly.

For AVC, use contact information published on assignmentvenuecentre.me. A copied AVC logo on Facebook, Telegram, WhatsApp or a PDF does not prove that the communication came from AVC.

3. Vacancy red flags

Guaranteed selection without meaningful screening

Some genuine entry-level jobs use simple screening, but “100% guaranteed job and visa” is a warning sign. Final selection, visa approval and deployment depend on parties and processes that a sourcing business cannot guarantee.

Salary far above the role with no explanation

A high salary can be real, but compare it with the job level, location, required experience and employer. Fraudulent offers often use an unusually attractive salary to reduce questions about the process.

Job title keeps changing

If you applied as an electrician but the offer shows helper, cleaner or labourer, do not accept “same visa, same work” without a clear authorized explanation and corrected documentation.

No identifiable employer or project owner

“Big company in Dubai” is not enough. Obtain the actual employer or legally responsible entity. A project name alone is not proof that the project is hiring you.

4. Payment red flags

Payment risk is separate from job genuineness. Even if a vacancy exists, a fraudster can insert themselves between the candidate and a genuine process.

  • Payment requested to an unrelated personal UPI/bank account without written explanation.
  • No receipt or invoice route.
  • Fee purpose changes after payment.
  • Refund terms are hidden or invented only after a problem occurs.
  • The candidate is told an official government/medical fee must be paid to a private person with no traceable basis.
  • The sender demands card OTP, banking password or remote access to the phone.
Never share authentication secretsNo legitimate recruiter needs your bank password, ATM PIN, card PIN or OTP to receive a recruitment-related payment.

Government and MEA guidance for overseas employment emphasizes checking the Recruiting Agent and obtaining proper receipts. Always check current official fee rules for the applicable route rather than relying on forwarded screenshots.

5. Document red flags

Identity documents are valuable even when no money is stolen. A suspicious actor can use passport and Aadhaar copies for impersonation or other fraud.

  • Full passport/Aadhaar requested before the vacancy or recipient is identified.
  • Original passport demanded casually with no acknowledgement.
  • Blank forms sent for signature.
  • Request to send a signature image “for office use” without a specific document.
  • Request to alter date of birth, experience, profession or passport information to “fit the visa.”
  • Offer to manufacture an experience certificate or trade certificate.

A real process may require extensive documentation after selection. The key differences are purpose, timing and traceability.

6. How to verify a recruitment message received on WhatsApp

  1. Do not use only the number that contacted you. Find the company's website or official listing separately.
  2. Compare the exact number/domain. A one-digit difference matters.
  3. Ask for a vacancy reference. Match job title, salary, location and employer.
  4. Confirm the responsible RA/employer route. Verify authorization through official sources where required.
  5. Move important terms to a durable record. Email, written offer, receipt or contract is easier to verify than a disappearing voice note.

If the sender becomes aggressive because you are verifying, treat that behaviour as additional risk.

7. A five-step routine you can reuse for every overseas job

AVC 5-check routine

  1. Source: Where did the vacancy come from, and can I independently reach that source?
  2. Responsibility: Who is the foreign employer and who is the registered/authorized recruitment party?
  3. Terms: Are salary, role, duty and benefits consistent across messages and documents?
  4. Money: Who receives each payment, for what service, under what receipt/refund rule?
  5. Record: Do I have copies of the vacancy, offer, contract, receipts and key communication?

This routine takes longer than trusting a logo, but much less time than recovering from a fraudulent payment or compromised passport.

8. If you suspect fraud after sending money or documents

Preserve evidence before blocking the sender: phone number, profile details, UPI ID/bank account, transaction reference, messages, PDFs, voice notes, advertisements and website links. Contact your bank/payment provider promptly where relevant.

For overseas-employment complaints involving Recruiting Agents or intending emigrants, MEA guidance points candidates to eMigrate/Protector of Emigrants channels. For cyber-enabled financial fraud, use the current official Indian cybercrime reporting channels as applicable. Do not rely on a social-media commenter who promises to recover money for another advance fee.

If someone is misusing AVC branding, send the suspicious communication to AVC through the official contact route listed on this website. Do not send passwords, OTPs or unnecessary sensitive records in the report.
