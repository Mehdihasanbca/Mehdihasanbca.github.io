Why Bihar Workers Are Frequently Targeted

Every year, hardworking youths from Darbhanga, Madhubani, Samastipur, Sitamarhi, and across North Bihar dream of supporting their families by working in the Middle East. Unfortunately, this aspiration is frequently preyed upon by unregistered middlemen, village touts, and fake recruitment agencies.

Because candidates are often unfamiliar with MEA licensing regulations, international visa verification portals, or the Emigration Act 1983, scammers exploit their trust to steal millions of rupees in so-called “visa processing fees.” Memorize these 15 warning signs to safeguard your savings and future.

15 Critical Red Flags of Recruitment Fraud

1. Demanding Upfront Money for “Registration” or “Interview Booking”

Legitimate recruitment agencies and support centers never charge candidates to appear for interviews. Under the Emigration Act 1983, charging job seekers upfront fees before an employment visa is issued is a punishable criminal offense.

2. Promising Salaries Two to Three Times Above Market Rate

If an offer promises SAR 4,500 or AED 5,000 for a general electrician or helper without supervisory credentials, it is a deliberate hook to make you drop your guard.

3. No Physical Office Address You Can Visit

Fraudsters operate through ephemeral WhatsApp groups, Facebook pages, or meet candidates in tea stalls or hotel lobbies. If an agency refuses to provide a verifiable physical commercial address, break contact immediately.

4. Inability to Produce an MEA Recruiting Agent (RA) License Number

Only agencies holding an active MEA license (format: B-XXXX/STATE/COM/1000+/YEAR) are legally authorized to deploy Indian workers abroad. Verify their license directly on mea.gov.in.

5. Offering “Direct Selection” Without a Trade Test or Interview

Major Gulf EPC contractors (Aramco contractors, ADNOC partners, QatarEnergy vendors) do not hire skilled tradesmen based solely on photo selection. If an agent promises a visa without assessing your trade skills, the offer is fabricated.

6. Unofficial Email Addresses and Poorly Formatted Letters

Legitimate corporate employers use official domains (e.g., hr@companyname.com), not free accounts like @gmail.com or @yahoo.com. Beware of letters riddled with spelling errors, blurry logos, or mismatched corporate seals.

7. Offering a Visit, Tourist, or Commercial Visa for Work

Working on a tourist or visit visa is strictly illegal in all GCC countries. If an agent tells you, “Go on a visit visa and the company will convert it in Dubai,” refuse. Many workers are stranded without legal rights or medical coverage.

8. Withholding Your Original Passport as Leverage

Agents have no legal right to confiscate your original passport. Never leave your passport without a formal written receipt.

9. Demanding Payment into Personal UPI or Individual Bank Accounts

Fraudulent operators instruct candidates to send funds via PhonePe or Google Pay to personal numbers rather than a registered corporate current account.

10. Directing You to Unaccredited Medical Testing Clinics

Gulf country work visas strictly require medical screening from GAMCA/Wafid-accredited centers booked via wafid.com. Fake clinics charge exorbitant rates for meaningless papers.

11. Promising “100% Guaranteed Visas in 48 Hours”

Legitimate embassy and governmental visa issuance takes structured timelines (typically 2 to 6 weeks). No recruiter can guarantee instantaneous governmental approvals.

12. Prohibiting You from Contacting the Destination Employer

If an agent becomes aggressive when you state you intend to call the overseas employer to verify the job mandate, they are actively concealing deception.

13. Pressure Tactics: “Pay within 1 Hour or Your Seat Goes to Someone Else”

Scammers create artificial urgency to force emotional, hasty financial decisions before you can consult family or advisors.

14. Proposing Indirect Travel Routing (Nepal, Oman, or Sri Lanka)

Agents offering roundabout flights to bypass Indian airport emigration controls are engaging in illegal migrant smuggling.

15. Refusing to Issue an Official GST Tax Invoice for Payments

Legitimate agencies issue tax invoices with valid GSTIN details. Cash-only demands without receipts indicate illicit operation.

How to Report Fraudulent Agents and Recover Funds

If you or someone you know has been cheated by an unauthorized middleman:

  1. Call the MEA Pravasi Bharatiya Sahayata Kendra (PBSK): Toll-free helpline 1800-11-3090 (24x7 assistance).
  2. Lodge an eMigrate Grievance: Register a formal complaint at emigrate.gov.in against the agent or agency name.
  3. File a Police Complaint: Submit an FIR at your local police station or Cyber Crime Police Station with WhatsApp chats, call recordings, UPI transaction IDs, and fake offer letters.
