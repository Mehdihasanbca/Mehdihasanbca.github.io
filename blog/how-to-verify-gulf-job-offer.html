How to Verify a Gulf Job Offer — 12 Steps Checklist

Before you pay anyone or travel anywhere, run through this 12-point verification checklist. It has protected hundreds of Bihar workers from fraud.

Why Every Gulf Job Offer Must Be Verified

Every year, thousands of workers from Bihar and other Indian states lose money — sometimes their life savings — to fraudulent Gulf job offers. The offers arrive via WhatsApp, phone calls, or through middlemen who appear legitimate. The only protection is a systematic verification routine before any money changes hands or any travel takes place.

This checklist is the same routine AVC uses internally when a candidate brings a job offer to our Darbhanga office. It is free to use. There is no special expertise required — just time and the willingness to ask questions.

Key rule: If any step in this checklist fails, stop the process immediately. A legitimate employer and a licensed recruiter will never object to verification. Pressure to skip steps is itself a fraud signal.

The 12-Step Verification Checklist

  1. Step 1 — Verify the Recruiting Agent license on MEA portal Visit mea.gov.in/ras.htm and search the agent's name or license number. Only MEA-licensed Recruiting Agents may legally place Indian workers in Gulf countries. If the agent is not listed, the placement is illegal under the Emigration Act 1983. This single check eliminates a large majority of fraudulent operators.
  2. Step 2 — Call the company directly (not the number given by the agent) Find the employer's official contact independently — from their website, LinkedIn page, or the destination country's business registry — and call or email them. Ask whether they are currently hiring for the specific role and whether the named agent is their authorized representative. Do not rely solely on the agent's version of this confirmation.
  3. Step 3 — Verify the offer letter format A genuine Gulf job offer letter will have: official company letterhead with full registered address and phone number; a wet or digital stamp; a signature from an authorized HR or management representative; specific job title, duty station, salary in local currency, and joining date. Reject any offer on plain paper, with generic fonts, no stamp, or with inconsistent formatting.
  4. Step 4 — Check that the salary is realistic Research the market salary for your trade in the destination country using Gulf job portals (Naukrigulf, Bayt, LinkedIn). If the offer promises a salary two to three times above market rate, treat it as a scam indicator. Fraudsters use inflated salaries to attract victims. A real offer matches market benchmarks closely.
  5. Step 5 — Confirm the visa category is a work visa Your visa must be a work visa (employment visa), not a visit visa, tourist visa, or any other category. Working on a visit visa is illegal in all Gulf countries and results in deportation and blacklisting. Ask the agent to show you the visa category in writing before you agree to travel.
  6. Step 6 — Never pay any fee before the visa stamp is in your passport Under Indian law and Ministry of External Affairs guidelines, Recruiting Agents may charge a maximum service fee only after a work visa is stamped in your passport. Any demand for upfront payment — for "processing," "training," "medical booking," "documentation," or any other reason — is illegal and a major fraud signal. Do not pay.
  7. Step 7 — Verify the GAMCA medical center on the official Wafid list Gulf country embassies only accept medical fitness certificates from approved GAMCA centers. Before booking your medical, verify the center's name on the Wafid portal (wafid.com). Do not attend a medical at any center that is not on this list — the result will not be accepted and you will have wasted money.
  8. Step 8 — Ask for the employment contract in writing before traveling You are entitled to receive and review a written employment contract before you board any flight. The contract must specify: role title, duties, monthly salary, working hours, overtime rate, accommodation and food arrangements, annual leave, return airfare entitlement, and termination conditions. Do not travel without this document in hand.
  9. Step 9 — Verify the company on the destination country's business registry Most Gulf countries have publicly searchable business registries. In the UAE, search the Department of Economic Development portal. In Saudi Arabia, use the Ministry of Commerce company search. In Qatar, use the Ministry of Commerce and Industry portal. Verify that the company exists, is active, and that the registered name matches what is on the offer letter.
  10. Step 10 — Check reviews on LinkedIn, Google, and Gulf expat forums Search the company name along with terms like "review," "employee experience," or "salary" on LinkedIn and Google. Check Gulf expat forums and Facebook groups for your trade. Look for patterns: multiple complaints about unpaid salaries, different job conditions on arrival, or threats to withhold passports are serious warning signs.
  11. Step 11 — Inform your local police station Write a simple letter to your local police station stating your name, destination, employer, and expected travel date. This creates a safety record and establishes your whereabouts if anything goes wrong. This is a precautionary step — not a formal complaint. It takes fifteen minutes and costs nothing.
  12. Step 12 — Register on the eMigrate portal before travel If your passport is ECR (Emigration Check Required), eMigrate registration is mandatory under Indian law. Even if you hold an ECNR passport, registration on emigrate.gov.in before departure is strongly recommended. It ensures the Government of India can assist you quickly if you encounter problems abroad and entitles you to consular support.

What to Do After Completing the Checklist

If all 12 steps pass, you have a substantially higher-confidence offer. You can then proceed with AVC's standard candidate registration process — which is free, documented, and linked to a licensed Recruiting Agent route.

If any step fails, do not proceed. Contact the MEA Pravasi Helpline on 1800-11-3090 (toll-free) to report the suspicious offer, or report it at mea.gov.in. You can also bring the offer to our Darbhanga office and we will help you identify what is wrong with it.
